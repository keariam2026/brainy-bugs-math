🌸 What Is a Shape?

A shape is the form or outline of an object. We see shapes everywhere: in our homes, schools, toys, buildings, and nature. Learning about shapes helps us understand the world around us.

🎀 Part 1: 2D Shapes

What are 2D shapes?

2D means two-dimensional. These shapes have length and width, but no thickness. They are flat shapes that we can draw on paper.

🔴

1. Circle

A circle is round and has no straight sides or corners.

Example: A coin or a clock.

Remember: 0 corners.

🟦

2. Square

A square has four equal sides and four right-angle corners.

Example: A square tile.

Remember: 4 equal sides.

▰

3. Rectangle

A rectangle has four sides and four right angles. Opposite sides are equal.

Example: A book or a door.

Remember: 2 pairs of equal sides.

🔺

4. Triangle

A triangle has three straight sides and three corners.

Example: A triangular road sign.

Remember: 3 sides.

💎

5. Rhombus

A rhombus has four equal sides. Opposite angles are equal.

Example: A diamond-shaped pattern.

Remember: 4 equal sides.

⬡

6. Hexagon

A hexagon is a polygon with six straight sides and six corners.

Example: A honeycomb cell.

Remember: 6 sides.

⬠

7. Pentagon

A pentagon is a polygon with five sides and five corners.

Example: The outline of a typical five-sided shape.

Remember: 5 sides.

🥚

8. Oval

An oval is a curved, rounded shape that looks like a stretched circle.

Example: An egg outline.

Remember: No straight sides.

💡 What Is a Polygon?

A polygon is a closed, flat shape made from straight line segments. Triangles, squares, pentagons, and hexagons are all polygons. A circle is not a polygon because it has no straight sides.

🩵 Part 2: 3D Shapes

What are 3D shapes?

3D means three-dimensional. These shapes have length, width, and height, so they take up space. Many real objects around us are 3D shapes.

⚽

1. Sphere

A sphere is completely round, like a ball.

Example: A football or a marble.

Remember: It has one curved surface.

🎲

2. Cube

A cube has six equal square faces, twelve edges, and eight vertices.

Example: A dice.

Remember: All edges are equal.

🥫

3. Cylinder

A cylinder has two flat circular faces and one curved surface.

Example: A tin can.

Remember: Two circular ends.

🍦

4. Cone

A cone has one circular base and one pointed top.

Example: An ice-cream cone.

Remember: One vertex at the tip.

📦

5. Cuboid

A cuboid has six rectangular faces, twelve edges, and eight vertices.

Example: A shoebox.

Remember: Box-shaped.

🔺

6. Pyramid

A pyramid has a base and triangular faces that meet at a point.

Example: An Egyptian pyramid with a square base.

Remember: Triangular faces meet at the top.

✏️ Part 4: Perimeter and Area

What is perimeter?

Perimeter is the total distance around the outside of a 2D shape. To find it, add the lengths of all its sides.

What is area?

Area is the amount of flat space inside a 2D shape. Area is measured in square units, such as cm².

Square

Perimeter = 4 × side

Area = side × side

Rectangle

Perimeter = 2 × (length + width)

Area = length × width

Triangle

Perimeter = side 1 + side 2 + side 3

Area = ½ × base × height

🌷 Example to Remember

A square has a side length of 4 cm.

Perimeter = 4 × 4 = 16 cm

Area = 4 × 4 = 16 cm²

Notice that perimeter uses ordinary length units, while area uses square units.
