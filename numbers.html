💜 What Are Numbers?

Numbers are symbols we use to represent quantities, amounts, and values. We use numbers when counting toys, measuring height, telling time, and buying things.

🌷 Part 1: Counting Numbers

Learn to Count

Counting helps us find out how many objects there are. We usually begin with 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, and continue in order. Zero (0) means there are no objects in a group.

012 345 678 910

🔢

Natural Numbers

Natural numbers are used for counting: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, and so on.

0️⃣

Zero

Zero represents no quantity. If you have three cookies and eat all three, you have zero left.

🔟

Whole Numbers

Whole numbers include zero and all positive counting numbers: 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, and so on.

🩵 Part 2: Place Value

What Is Place Value?

Place value tells us what a digit is worth based on its position in a number. Moving left, each place is worth ten times the place to its right.

Number Hundreds Tens Ones
352 3 = 300 5 = 50 2 = 2
417 4 = 400 1 = 10 7 = 7
806 8 = 800 0 = 0 6 = 6

✨ Example: Number 352

3 hundreds = 300

5 tens = 50

2 ones = 2

Expanded form: 352 = 300 + 50 + 2

🎀 Part 3: Odd and Even Numbers

2️⃣ 4️⃣ 6️⃣

Even Numbers

Even numbers can be divided by 2 without leaving a remainder.

Examples: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12.

1️⃣ 3️⃣ 5️⃣

Odd Numbers

Odd numbers leave a remainder of 1 when divided by 2.

Examples: 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11.

💡 Easy Trick

Look at the last digit. Numbers ending in 0, 2, 4, 6, or 8 are even.

Numbers ending in 1, 3, 5, 7, or 9 are odd.

Example: 248 is even, but 357 is odd.

🌈 Part 4: Comparing Numbers

💗

Greater Than ( > )

Means one number is larger than another.

Example: 8 > 3

💙

Less Than ( < )

Means one number is smaller than another.

Example: 2 < 7

💜

Equal To ( = )

Means two values are the same.

Example: 5 = 5

Remember!

When comparing two whole numbers, the number with more digits is larger. If they have the same number of digits, compare the digits from left to right.

Example: 425 > 398 because 4 hundreds is more than 3 hundreds.

🧮 Part 5: Basic Operations

➕

Addition

Addition combines quantities to find a total.

Example: 3 + 2 = 5

➖

Subtraction

Subtraction takes one quantity away from another.

Example: 7 − 4 = 3

✖️

Multiplication

Multiplication is repeated addition of equal groups.

Example: 3 × 4 = 12

➗

Division

Division shares a quantity into equal groups.

Example: 12 ÷ 3 = 4

📖 Part 6: Practice Examples

Example 1: Addition

You have 4 pencils and get 3 more.

4 + 3 = 7 pencils

Example 2: Subtraction

You have 9 candies and give away 2.

9 − 2 = 7 candies left

Example 3: Multiplication

There are 3 bags with 2 apples in each bag.

3 × 2 = 6 apples

Example 4: Division

Share 8 cookies equally among 4 children.

8 ÷ 4 = 2 cookies each
